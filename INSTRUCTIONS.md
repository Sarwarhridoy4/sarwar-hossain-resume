## 8. Resume Structure and Writing Standards

Create a clean, professional resume with the following sections:

1. Name and contact information.
2. Professional headline.
3. Professional summary.
4. Technical skills.
5. Professional experience, in reverse chronological order.
6. Selected projects.
7. Education.
8. Certifications, if verified.

Include education and certifications only after obtaining accurate information. If those details are missing, leave appropriate placeholders in the working draft and ask me for confirmation before producing the final version.

### Professional Experience

For each role:

- Show the employer, position, employment type where relevant, and dates.
- Use concise, action-oriented bullet points.
- Emphasize engineering contributions, implementation details, and ownership.
- Avoid repeating the same technology list in every bullet.
- Include quantified achievements only when supported by evidence.

### Selected Projects

For each project:

- Show its name and a short description.
- Identify the technologies used.
- Explain the most important implementation or engineering contribution.
- Include a verifiable result when available.
- Add the source-code or live-demo link where appropriate.

### Writing Style

Use clear, professional English suitable for international technical recruiters.

Use strong action verbs such as Designed, Developed, Implemented, Integrated, Automated, Deployed, Optimized, and Troubleshot.

Use past tense for completed responsibilities and present tense for ongoing responsibilities where appropriate.

Do not use first-person pronouns in resume bullets.

## 9. ATS Optimization

Make the resume compatible with applicant tracking systems used by software companies and recruitment platforms.

Requirements:

- Use a conventional, single-column layout.
- Use standard section headings such as Professional Summary, Technical Skills, Professional Experience, Projects, and Education.
- Use selectable text and simple formatting.
- Avoid tables, multiple columns, graphics, icons, photographs, skill bars, and decorative elements in the ATS version.
- Include relevant technical keywords naturally.
- Avoid keyword stuffing and repetitive descriptions.
- Maintain consistent job titles, company names, date formats, and punctuation.
- Keep the primary resume to one or two pages, depending on the amount of relevant experience.
- Do not remove meaningful professional experience merely to force the resume onto one page.

Optimize primarily for Full Stack Software Engineer and Software Engineer positions, while keeping the content adaptable to backend and DevOps-oriented roles.
